# Go-to-Market

## General strategy

The general strategy has to be carved in a stone!

We are not doing anything that is not moving us towards our goals.
The main goal for now is to secure the first client.
We need to concentrate all our efforts on that.
If it's a CTA, it has to be very very specific, unique and valuable. No time for bullshit like Book a call. Nobody wants to book a call just to book a call. If someone decides to spend time on you they should be 100% sure they get something important there. That's what it should be.

Everything what we do should be like a punch.
- 1st punch our headline - right to the point, no blablabla, just hit right into the target.
- 2nd punch our value proposition - right to the vital organs, where it definitely hurt, just right there without any ado.
- 3d punch our services - just into your weakest spot as if we were studying you for months - offer that cannot be refused
- 4th punch CTA - right to the point, exactly to where it hurts already and KO - we have connect request

## Messaging Principles

**Headline philosophy**: For the 2-second hook, the visitor doesn't care about your philosophy. They care about THEIR problem. SO, it has to be a clean site, and a big headline that speaks to THEM, not about US.

**Subhead philosophy**: The subhead should reinforce the headline with a concrete promise or proof point. Not a company description. This is a reason to keep reading.

**CTA philosophy**: A CTA should feels natural, with low-pressure. The visitor should feel they're getting something valuable, not committing to a sales process.

**Content philosophy**: Content is specific to their domain. No hype, but value.

## Website as Salesperson

The website must be a main sales point. Desired impression sequence:
1. **2 seconds**: Visually credible and worth giving it a chance
2. **3 seconds**: Content looks relevant and hooks the visitor
3. **5 seconds**: "Interesting, let's see what they actually offer"
4. **20 seconds**: "These might be the right people for our AI project"
5. **30 seconds**: "It shouldn't harm to leave a talk request with them" and reacts to CTA

Success test: A VP-level visitor says to a colleague, "I found some guys that we can add to the pool of vendors to review for our project."

## Primary CTA

**Button**: "Discuss Your AI Project"
**Subtext**: "Get an honest assessment of feasibility, timeline, and cost directly from our chief architect."
**Founder photo** shown next to the form.

Why this wording:

| Considered | Problem |
|---|---|
| "Contact Us" | Too vague. Says nothing about what happens next. |
| "Get Started" | Too salesy. Implies commitment. |
| "Book a Free Strategy Session" | Only works for explorers. A CTO comparing vendors wants to describe their project, not get a "session." |
| "Estimate Project" | Contradicts our methodology — we can't estimate before we validate. Invites comparison shopping: a VP clicking "Estimate" on 5 vendor sites is building a spreadsheet, not choosing a partner. We lose before the conversation starts. |

"Discuss Your AI Project" works for all three visitor types: explorers (is this feasible?), buyers (here's what I need), researchers (give me something for my boss). The subtext puts "estimate" in the promise without making it the action.

The AI Strategy Session offer lives on the Services page — it's an entry point into our methodology, not the main homepage CTA. A visitor who clicks "Discuss Your AI Project" may get routed to a Strategy Session based on their needs.

## Sales Pipeline

Currently nonexistent — critical gap that must be built. Key open questions:
- What happens after the first form submission? (founder responds within 24h, but then what?)
- How to qualify: route to AI Strategy Session vs Discovery vs direct scoping?
- What minimum information should the founder capture before the first real conversation?
- How to track inquiries without a CRM?
- At what point does a CRM become necessary?

Draft answers:

1. **Inbound** → Form at info@remilink.com
2. **Qualify** (within 4h) → Founder scans: ICP match? Complexity level? Urgency?
   - *Draft criteria: 100+ employees, complex product/operations, mentions timeline/budget, not "just exploring AI for fun"*
3. **First Response** (within 24h) → Architect responds personally:
   - "Here's our initial take + what we'd do" (if enough info)
   - "Can we do a 2h call to understand this better?" (if complex)
   - "Honestly, this isn't our strength — here's who might help" (if not ICP)
4. **Diagnose** (free 2h call) → Architect-led. Understand the real problem. Determine entry point.
5. **Route** → Based on diagnosis:
   - AI Strategy Session (paid, 1 week) — needs deeper assessment
   - Discovery & Blueprint — problem clear, scope isn't
   - Concept Validation — knows what they want, needs proof
6. **Proposal** → Scope, timeline, pricing based on diagnosis.
7. **Close** → Sign, kick off.

**Tracking**: Spreadsheet (lead, date, status, next action) until deal flow justifies CRM. Likely 10-20 inquiries.
**Disqualification**: Not every lead gets a free 2h call. Quick email exchange reveals fit. Skip: no budget signal, <50 employees, no AI angle.

## Pricing Display Rules

**Principle**: "Be transparent about the steps and the entry cost, let the conversation handle the rest."

- **Show on site**: Entry costs and monthly rates
- **Don't show**: Project totals or durations next to prices
- **Don't show**: Individual engineer rates
- **Why**: "$15K/mo" feels manageable. "$60K total" invites comparison shopping. Same money, different psychology. Durations live in the methodology section, prices in the pricing section — never in the same table cell.

These rules apply across all channels: website, presentations, proposals, LinkedIn content.
